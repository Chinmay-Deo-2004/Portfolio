RDSE: Real-Time Directional Speech Enhancement Framework for Dual-Mic Commodity Smartphones

Research summary

Abstract

Audio-visual zooming on commodity smartphones is extremely challenging due to limited microphone count, restricted computational resources and real-world acoustic aberrations like noise, interference and reverberation. We present RDSE (Real-Time Directional Speech Enhancement), a lightweight audio enhancement framework designed for near real-time single speaker separation for a user-specified direction on dual-microphone commodity smartphones. The proposed framework combines U-Net based dereverberation, a beamformer with a temporal convolutional network and MetricGAN based denoising in a cascaded architecture. To enable systematic evaluation, we introduce SpARZ100, a controlled dataset that separately captures anechoic and reverberant conditions. Experimental results demonstrate competitive objective performance, while controlled evaluation on SpARZ100 demonstrates the effectiveness of the modular pipeline. Practical feasibility of the proposed approach was also validated through a real-time live-streaming deployment highlighting its potential for low-cost, real-time directional speech enhancement.
