INSPECT · 2025

Published
View Paper ↗ View Source Code ↗

Research summary

Abstract

This paper presents a complete pipeline for estimating rooftop solar potential from high-resolution satellite imagery using deep learning and irradiance modeling. U-Net and U-Net++ architectures are implemented for building footprint segmentation, trained on the Massachusetts Buildings Dataset and evaluated using standard segmentation metrics. While U-Net++ achieves slightly higher test-set scores (e.g., Dice and IoU), U-Net demonstrates superior generalization on unseen urban imagery—detecting more usable, fragmented rooftops and yielding significantly higher solar energy estimates. This highlights the importance of domain-specific evaluation over purely pixel-level metrics. The final output is a georeferenced rooftop-level solar potential heatmap, generated using the pvlib library. The U-Net model is deployed via a lightweight, browser-based interface using Streamlit, enabling non-expert users to upload satellite imagery and obtain solar predictions without GIS tools. This work demonstrates the feasibility of integrating deep learning, geospatial processing, and solar modeling into a scalable, accessible framework for urban energy planning and smart grid deployment.
